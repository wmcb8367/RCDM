Because of these same fundamentals, if you find yourself farther back in the fleet on an oscillating day, the gate becomes one of the best passing lanes on the race course. Option A as you come into the gate should be the same as the leaders: in low traffic situations, you'll round the favored gate and try to tack immediately. If you can execute a good round-and-tack maneuver, you'll often be able to sneak inside of teams who rounded, sailed for a minute, and then tacked, which will set you up to gain on the next shift. Interestingly, option B can also be a good opportunity to gain if you recognize that there is traffic at the favored gate. In option B, the move is to round the unfavored gate in less traffic, and hang straight to stay on the lifted tack. By executing a slightly simpler maneuver, you'll have a quicker escape from the mark, and you'll gain some leverage into the next shift, which should be a gain as you continue up the race course.

In reality, this leeward gate will almost always be dictated largely by traffic, so the main priority to keep in mind on the second beat, is to get lifted as quickly as possible in the lane that you deserve!
